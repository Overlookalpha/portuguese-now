⏰ Lesson 15

Time and Schedule

Learn how to tell the time, ask about schedules and make appointments in Brazilian Portuguese.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • ✅ Tell the time in Portuguese.
  • ✅ Ask what time something happens.
  • ✅ Talk about appointments.
  • ✅ Understand schedules.
  • ✅ Use common time expressions.

📖 Before We Start

Knowing how to tell the time is essential when traveling, working, studying or meeting friends in Brazil. Brazilians usually use the 12-hour clock in conversations but also understand the 24-hour format.

💬 Examples

Que horas são? → What time is it?

São três horas. → It's three o'clock.

Meu compromisso é às oito. → My appointment is at eight.

O banco abre às nove. → The bank opens at nine.

A loja fecha às seis. → The store closes at six.

Nos vemos amanhã. → See you tomorrow.

🕒 How to Tell Time

In Brazilian Portuguese, we normally use the verb ser to tell the time. Use É for one o'clock and São for all other hours.

Portuguese English
É uma hora. It's one o'clock.
São duas horas. It's two o'clock.
São cinco horas. It's five o'clock.
São dez horas. It's ten o'clock.
É meio-dia. It's noon.
É meia-noite. It's midnight.

🗣️ Dialogue

Maria: Que horas são?

Lucas: São três horas da tarde.

Maria: Que horas começa a reunião?

Lucas: Às quatro horas.

Maria: Temos tempo então.

Lucas: Sim, vamos tomar um café primeiro.

💡 Pronunciation Tip

The letter R in horário sounds stronger at the beginning of the word. Practice saying:

Hora
Horário
Relógio
Reunião

🇧🇷 Brazilian Culture Tip

Brazilians usually say things like "às três" instead of "às três horas" in everyday conversations. It's also common to hear people say "daqui a pouco", which literally means "in a little while," but the exact amount of time depends on the context.
